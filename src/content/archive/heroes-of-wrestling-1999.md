On 10 October 1999, a new company called Heroes of Wrestling broadcast its first pay-per-view from Casino Magic in Bay St. Louis, Mississippi. The card was built on wrestlers who had been stars in the 1980s and early 1990s. It was the company's only pay-per-view, and the _Wrestling Observer_ named it the worst major wrestling event of 1999.

## The Concept

The idea was to sell nostalgia: well-known names from wrestling's boom years, presented together on one show. The main event was billed around Jake "The Snake" Roberts, Yokozuna, King Kong Bundy and Jim Neidhart, although sources differ on the exact match line-up, which changed during planning.

## The Failure

The execution collapsed. Many of the performers were years past their best and had not been prepared for the matches they were booked in. Roberts was in no condition to perform as planned, and his on-air interview and behaviour during the main event became the most remembered part of the broadcast. Yokozuna's physical condition was also widely noted in reviews. The main event ended in confusion, and the broadcast ended abruptly.

This is a **Technical Failure**: the concept had an audience, but the show fell apart through poor planning, a lack of preparation and a failure to take account of the performers' condition.

## Lessons for the Craft

The event is a case study in why a promotion cannot rely on famous names alone. A responsible promoter needs to know whether the wrestlers it books are fit and ready to perform, both for the quality of the show and for the performers' own wellbeing. Some of the performers were dealing with serious health problems, and Roberts with addiction, which is part of why the event is now remembered with sympathy as well as criticism. Roberts later spoke publicly about his recovery.

For several weeks in late 1990, the World Wrestling Federation (WWF) displayed a giant egg at its shows and on television, inviting fans to guess what was inside. The egg hatched at Survivor Series, broadcast on Thanksgiving night, 22 November 1990, from the Hartford Civic Center in Connecticut.

Out climbed the Gobbledy Gooker: Héctor Guerrero, a respected wrestler and member of the Guerrero family, in a full turkey costume.

## The Failure

The build-up had raised expectations of a major new star. Gene Okerlund, who hosted the hatching, had speculated about what the egg might hold. When the Gooker appeared, the crowd fell silent and then booed, and the following dance with Okerlund did nothing to win them round. Commentator Roddy Piper's insistence that the Gooker had "won the heart of Hartford" only underlined how badly it had gone. The character was quickly dropped.

The idea misjudged its audience, and the reveal could not match weeks of mystery. The registry files it under the charter's "So Bad It's Good" exception, because of what happened next.

## Why It Endures

The Gobbledy Gooker has become one of the most famous failures in wrestling history and a fond punchline for fans. The WWF itself later embraced the joke, bringing the character back for a "Gimmick Battle Royal" of nostalgic acts at WrestleMania X-Seven in 2001.

It remains a lesson in the risks of a long mystery build: the longer an audience waits, the more the payoff has to deliver.

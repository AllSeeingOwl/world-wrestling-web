WrestleMania 36 was broadcast over two nights, 4 and 5 April 2020, from WWE's Performance Center in Orlando, Florida, without a live audience because of the COVID-19 pandemic. Two of its matches were not filmed in a ring in front of cameras at all, but shot and edited like short films. This entry nominates them together as a composite work.

## The Matches

- **The Boneyard Match**, on night one, saw The Undertaker face AJ Styles in a graveyard set built on location in the Orlando area. The fight moved through the set with film-style camerawork, music and effects, and ended with The Undertaker burying Styles in a grave and riding away on his motorcycle.
- **The Firefly Fun House Match**, on night two, saw Bray Wyatt take John Cena through a surreal journey through Cena's own career, recreating scenes from his past in a dreamlike sequence. Wyatt won. The segments were recorded at WWE's headquarters in Stamford, Connecticut.

## Why It Matters

Without a crowd, the usual tools of a big match, the noise, the reactions, the sense of a shared event, were missing. The cinematic matches replaced them with the tools of film: editing, sets, music and visual storytelling.

Both were widely praised as highlights of WrestleMania 36, and other promotions followed with cinematic matches of their own during the pandemic. They also prompted a lasting debate about what counts as a wrestling match, which is part of why they are significant.

On 4 January 2017, in the main event of New Japan Pro-Wrestling's (NJPW) Wrestle Kingdom 11 at the Tokyo Dome, IWGP Heavyweight Champion Kazuchika Okada defended his title against Kenny Omega. Okada retained with a spinning tombstone piledriver followed by his Rainmaker lariat. It was, at the time, the longest match ever held on NJPW's annual 4 January Tokyo Dome show.

## The Context

Omega earned his title shot by winning the 2016 G1 Climax, defeating Hirooki Goto in the final. He was the first non-Japanese wrestler to win the tournament in its history. As leader of the Bullet Club, he was also the face of NJPW's growing international following.

Okada was the promotion's established ace, so the match set NJPW's homegrown champion against the outsider who had broken one of its traditions.

## The Match

The match built gradually over a long running time, with each wrestler trying and failing to land his signature finish. Omega's One-Winged Angel became the move the crowd waited for and never saw, while Okada repeatedly fought out of positions that looked hopeless. The finish rewarded the champion's resilience and left Omega's story unresolved.

## Why It Matters

Wrestle Kingdom 11 reached viewers around the world through NJPW's own streaming service, NJPW World, and was later broadcast in the United States on AXS TV. The match was widely discussed in English-language media and fan communities, and it showed that a Japanese promotion could reach a global audience directly, without a television partner in each country.

The match began one of the defining rivalries of the decade. Omega and Okada met several more times, and Omega won the IWGP Heavyweight Championship from Okada in a two-out-of-three-falls match at Dominion in June 2018. The international profile Omega built in New Japan contributed to his role in founding All Elite Wrestling (AEW) in 2019.

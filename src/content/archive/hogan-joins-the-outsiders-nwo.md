On 7 July 1996, at WCW's Bash at the Beach pay-per-view in Daytona Beach, Florida, Hulk Hogan revealed himself as the mystery third member of The Outsiders. Scott Hall and Kevin Nash had promised a partner for their match against Sting, Lex Luger and Randy Savage. When Hogan walked to the ring, the crowd assumed he had come to help WCW. Instead he dropped his leg on Savage and joined the invaders.

In the interview that followed, Hogan named the group the "New World Order".

## The Context

Hall and Nash had arrived in WCW from the WWF earlier in 1996. Nash debuted on _Monday Nitro_ on 10 June, and the pair were presented as outsiders invading WCW. The storyline drew power from its ambiguity: many viewers were unsure whether the invasion was real.

Hogan had been wrestling's most famous hero for more than a decade, and his clean-cut image had begun to feel dated. Turning him into a villain was a major gamble.

## Why It Matters

The heel turn remade Hogan as "Hollywood" Hogan and gave the nWo instant credibility. The storyline became the engine of WCW's success during the Monday Night Wars: _Monday Nitro_ beat the WWF's _Raw_ in the television ratings for more than 80 consecutive weeks, a streak that began in June 1996 and ended in April 1998.

The nWo also changed how wrestling looked and sounded. Its black-and-white branding, its T-shirts and its anti-authority attitude spread far beyond the wrestling audience, and the idea of a faction "invading" a promotion became one of the industry's most copied storylines.

Its legacy includes a warning. WCW leaned on the nWo for years, splitting it into rival factions and reviving it repeatedly, and that over-reliance is often cited in accounts of the company's decline, including in this registry's entry on the Fingerpoke of Doom.

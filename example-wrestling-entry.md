# The Montreal Screwjob: A Night That Changed Wrestling Forever

On November 9, 1997, the landscape of professional wrestling was irrevocably altered inside the Molson Centre in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. What was billed as a standard World Wrestling Federation (WWF) Championship defense between defending champion Bret "The Hitman" Hart and challenger Shawn Michaels instead culminated in one of the most controversial unscripted moments in sports entertainment history—an event now universally known as the "Montreal Screwjob."

## The Context

Heading into the 1997 _Survivor Series_, Bret Hart had secretly signed a lucrative contract with rival promotion World Championship Wrestling (WCW). WWF owner Vince McMahon, concerned that Hart would take the WWF Championship to WCW television, demanded that Hart drop the title to Michaels in Montreal. Hart, citing a creative control clause in his contract and his bitter real-life rivalry with Michaels, refused to lose the title in his home country of Canada, though he agreed to forfeit or lose the belt later.

## The Incident

During the climax of the match, Michaels applied Hart's own finishing maneuver, the Sharpshooter, on the champion. Without Hart ever submitting, referee Earl Hebner—acting under strict, secret orders from McMahon—abruptly signaled for the bell to ring, declaring Michaels the winner by submission.

The immediate aftermath was chaotic. A visibly furious Hart spat in McMahon's face, destroyed television equipment at ringside, and famously traced the letters "WCW" in the air. Michaels, allegedly feigning ignorance at the time, quickly retreated backstage with the championship.

## Historical Impact

The Montreal Screwjob shattered the carefully maintained illusion of professional wrestling. By breaking the "fourth wall" and acknowledging the scripted nature of the sport on live pay-per-view, McMahon inadvertently birthed the "Mr. McMahon" character—a tyrannical, evil boss that would become the primary antagonist of the wildly successful Attitude Era.

The incident not only propelled the WWF to unprecedented financial success but also served as a catalyst for a paradigm shift in how professional wrestling stories were told, permanently blurring the lines between reality and fiction.

On 14 November 1990, at All Japan Women's Pro-Wrestling's (AJW) annual Wrestlemarinepiad event in Yokohama, WWWA World Champion Bull Nakano defended her title against Aja Kong inside a steel cage with no referee. Nakano retained after roughly 21 minutes by climbing down from the cage. The match is still regarded as one of the most important in the history of women's wrestling.

## The Context

By 1990 Bull Nakano was AJW's top champion, known for her painted face, towering hairstyle and aggressive style. Aja Kong was rising quickly as one of the promotion's most powerful challengers. The cage stipulation, with no referee and victory by escape, removed the usual safety valves of a championship match and signalled that this would be a fight rather than a contest.

## The Match

The violence began before the cage was locked, with Kong attacking Nakano before she reached the ring. Weapons played a central role: Nakano used scissors on Kong, and Kong struck back with nunchaku. The defining moment came when Nakano climbed to the top of the cage and dropped her Guillotine Legdrop onto Kong, before escaping to win.

## Why It Matters

The match showed that women's wrestling could headline with the same intensity and storytelling as any men's match, at a time when it was rarely presented that way outside Japan. It helped set the tone for the joshi boom of the early 1990s, when AJW drew large crowds and produced many of the decade's most admired matches.

Both wrestlers went on to wider recognition. Aja Kong later became WWWA World Champion herself, and Bull Nakano won the WWF Women's Championship in 1994. Nakano was inducted into the WWE Hall of Fame in 2024.

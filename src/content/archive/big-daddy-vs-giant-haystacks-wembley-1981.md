On 18 June 1981, at Wembley Arena in London, Big Daddy faced Giant Haystacks in the most anticipated match of British wrestling's television era. Big Daddy won, and the match was shown on ITV's _World of Sport_ later that month.

## The Context

From the late 1970s, Big Daddy (Shirley Crabtree) was the biggest star in British wrestling: a cheerful, enormous hero in a sequinned top hat, beloved by children and families. Giant Haystacks (Martin Ruane), even larger and bearded, was his natural opposite, and the two had been feuding for years before Wembley.

Wrestling was a fixture of ITV's _World of Sport_ on Saturday afternoons. For many British households it was a weekly ritual, and its stars were household names.

## Why It Matters

The match represents the peak of wrestling's place in British popular culture. Audience figures of 16 to 18 million are often quoted for its broadcast, although these are not supported by official ratings data for 1981, and the true figure is uncertain. Even so, the feud was among the best-known television rivalries in the country.

The match also illustrates the limits of the era's approach. Big Daddy's matches were deliberately short and simple, built around his popularity rather than athletic contests, and critics have argued that this style, and the reliance on a small number of ageing stars, contributed to British wrestling's decline through the 1980s. ITV dropped wrestling from its schedules in 1988.

_Santo vs. las Mujeres Vampiro_ ("Santo versus the Vampire Women") is a Mexican horror-action film directed by Alfonso Corona Blake, which premiered in Mexico City on 11 October 1962. It stars El Santo, the masked luchador, as himself: a wrestler who is also a superhero, called on to defeat a cult of vampire women.

It is the best-known of the dozens of films El Santo made from the 1950s onwards, and this entry represents that body of work.

## The Homage

El Santo, whose real name was Rodolfo Guzmán Huerta, was already one of Mexico's most famous wrestlers. His films built on that fame. In them he never removes his silver mask, fights monsters, mad scientists and criminals, and often breaks off from the plot to wrestle a match in the ring.

The films borrow wrestling's visual language and its stories of good and evil directly, and mix them with horror and science fiction. They meet the charter's criteria for Homage & Influence through both aesthetic influence and narrative structure.

## Why It Matters

The Santo films made the masked wrestler a fixture of Mexican popular culture, alongside comic books that carried the same character. They also shaped how the rest of the world saw lucha libre. This film was dubbed into English and released in the United States as _Samson vs. the Vampire Women_, and it was later featured on _Mystery Science Theater 3000_ in 1995, introducing it to a new audience.

El Santo's mask became one of the most recognisable images in Mexico, and the tradition of the luchador as a masked hero, inside and outside the ring, owes much to his films. Later works such as _Nacho Libre_, also in this registry, draw on the same tradition.

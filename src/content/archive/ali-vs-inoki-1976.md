On 26 June 1976, at the Nippon Budokan in Tokyo, the world heavyweight boxing champion Muhammad Ali faced Antonio Inoki, the founder and top star of New Japan Pro-Wrestling, in a contest between a boxer and a wrestler. The bout went the full 15 rounds and was declared a draw.

## The Contest

The rules were negotiated specially for the event, and they limited what Inoki could do standing up. For most of the contest Inoki lay on his back on the mat and kicked at Ali's legs, while Ali threw very few punches. Inoki had points deducted for infractions, and the official scoring ended level. Ali's legs were badly damaged by the kicks, and he later developed blood clots.

## Why It Matters

At the time, the contest was widely mocked as a dull farce, and audiences who expected a spectacle were disappointed. Its importance became clear later.

The event drew huge worldwide attention to professional wrestling, and to Inoki in particular, at a time when Ali was one of the most famous people on earth. It also raised a question that would shape combat sports for decades: what happens when fighters from different disciplines meet under one set of rules? Historians of mixed martial arts often point to Ali–Inoki as an early step on the road to the sport.

The two men later became friends. Inoki adopted Ali's "Ali Bomaye" chant as his own entrance theme, and the contest remained a central part of Inoki's legacy in Japan.

# The Wrestling Heritage Registry Editorial Handbook

Welcome to the Wrestling Heritage Registry. This handbook outlines the editorial standards and guidelines for contributing to our professional wrestling registry. Our goal is to maintain a high-quality, historically accurate, and engaging archive of professional wrestling's rich history.

## 1. Core Principles

Our entries must uphold a standard of professionalism and accuracy.

- **Historical Accuracy:** Entries must be factually accurate accounts of real professional wrestling events. Double-check all dates, locations, match outcomes, and participants. Do not present rumors or unverified backstage gossip as fact unless specifically documenting the historical impact of such rumors (and clearly citing them as such).
- **Journalistic Tone:** Write like sports historians or respected wrestling journalists, not as fans (often referred to as "marks" in industry parlance). Avoid overly emotional language, extreme bias toward certain wrestlers, or "kayfabe" presentations (treating scripted storylines as literal reality, except when describing the narrative of the event itself). Maintain an objective, analytical perspective.
- **Significance Over Acclaim:** Focus on works that mattered, whether triumphant or tragic. A work does not need to be a "five-star match" to qualify; its significance is the only test. Ask yourself: "Why does this entry matter to the broader history of professional wrestling?"
- **Impartiality and Global Scope:** No promotion, era, style or country is favoured. Moments from promotions anywhere in the world, active or defunct, are equally welcome.

## 2. Eligibility

These rules come from section IV of the Registry Charter, which is on the About page (and in `charter.md` in the repository).

- **Five-year minimum:** A work must be at least five years old, counted from its original broadcast or period of activity, so its legacy can be properly judged. There is no maximum age.
- **Verifiable record:** The work must have been televised, live-streamed or otherwise distributed on a professional, publicly accessible platform.
- **Works, not people:** The registry records works (matches, promos, angles, events, business decisions and media), not wrestlers or careers. A performer's importance is shown through the works they appear in.
- **Expanded media:** Podcasts, YouTube series, Twitch channels, documentaries, interview series and investigative reporting from or about the wrestling industry are eligible when they had a demonstrable cultural or historical impact.
- **The "Universal Language" test:** For non-English matches, explain how the story and drama come across without translated commentary. Promos and angles are judged on their documented impact in their native context.
- **Nomination limit:** Each person may nominate up to fifty works in a nomination period.

## 3. Classification Rules

To ensure our archive is easily navigable and organized, entries must be classified appropriately.

- **Registry Section and Significance:** Every entry belongs to one registry section and has one type of significance from that section. Each section has its own colour on the site, shown here.
  - <span class="inline-block w-3 h-3 rounded-sm align-middle mr-1 bg-amber-400" aria-hidden="true"></span>**Registry of Excellence** _(gold)_: Cultural Significance (it entered the wider public consciousness), Historical Significance (it fundamentally changed the industry) or Aesthetic Significance (exceptional artistic and athletic merit).
  - <span class="inline-block w-3 h-3 rounded-sm align-middle mr-1 bg-rose-400" aria-hidden="true"></span>**Registry of Infamy, Division A (Failures in Craft)** _(rose)_: Conceptual Failure, Technical Failure, Contextual Failure, or the "So Bad It's Good" exception. These are case studies, not an invitation to mock.
  - <span class="inline-block w-3 h-3 rounded-sm align-middle mr-1 bg-red-500" aria-hidden="true"></span>**Registry of Infamy, Division B (The Cautionary Archive)** _(red)_: Exploitation of real-life tragedy, breaches of performer safety and trust, and systemic failures. This division is **not open to public nomination**; the Preservation Board chooses its inductees privately.
  - <span class="inline-block w-3 h-3 rounded-sm align-middle mr-1 bg-sky-400" aria-hidden="true"></span>**Registry of Homage & Influence** _(blue)_: Works from outside the wrestling industry (video games, animation, film and other media) with a clear, demonstrable element taken from or paying homage to wrestling.
- **Era Classification:** Group entries by decade (e.g., 1980s, 1990s, 2000s, 2010s); the era field accepts only a decade. Established historical eras (e.g., The Golden Era, The Monday Night Wars, The Attitude Era, The Ruthless Aggression Era, the joshi boom) are specific to one promotion or country, so name them in the article instead, where they give useful context.
- **Promotion or Creator:** Clearly identify the promotion(s) involved. Common categories include, but are not limited to: WWF/WWE, WCW, ECW, NWA, NJPW, AJPW, ROH, TNA/Impact, and AEW. For works outside a promotion, name the creator instead: the studio, publisher, network, channel or media outlet (e.g., "Namco" for _Tekken_).
- **People Involved:** List the key people in the work: wrestlers, plus owners, referees, commentators, hosts or creators where they matter. For Homage & Influence entries, list the wrestlers being referenced and the creators responsible.
- **Entry Types:**
  - **Single Work Entries:** A specific match, promo, angle, television segment, pay-per-view, business decision or piece of media. Detail the context leading up to it, what occurred, and the aftermath.
  - **Composite Work Entries:** A series of interconnected works (a match trilogy, a tournament, a complete feud) nominated as one entry because their collective significance is greater than the sum of their parts. Define the scope by listing each part, with its title, date and event, in the entry's composite-work fields (the submit form's "Composite work" section); the site shows them as a list on the entry page. The entry's date is the date of the first part.

## 4. Special Features

Certain types of historical moments require specific handling to fully capture their significance.

- **Cross-Promotion Moments:** When a wrestler or event spans multiple promotions (e.g., the WCW/ECW Invasion, the NWA Worlds Heavyweight Championship being defended in TNA, or the "Forbidden Door" crossover events), clearly document the participating entities. Explain the business or narrative context that allowed the crossover to occur.
- **Legacy Documentation:** Every entry should assess the work's lasting influence on the industry and on later generations of performers. For composite works, note any single component (a match, promo or segment) that stands out on its own, and propose it for a "Citation for Individual Merit" in the entry's fields. Only the Preservation Board can issue the citation, so the site presents it as a proposal.

## 5. Citation Requirements

Proper sourcing is essential for maintaining the credibility of the Wrestling Heritage Registry. All entries must include the following:

- **Source Material:** Indicate where the information originates. This could be a specific television broadcast, a pay-per-view event, a recognized wrestling database (e.g., Cagematch, ProFightDB), an official promotion website, or a verified shoot interview/autobiography.
- **Air Date / Event Date:** Provide the exact date (Month, Day, Year) the event occurred or was broadcast. If the event was taped prior to airing, provide both the taping date and the broadcast date if relevant.
- **Promotion and Event Name:** Clearly state the promotion (or creator) and the name of the show or work (e.g., "WWE WrestleMania X-Seven," "WCW Monday Nitro," "NJPW Wrestle Kingdom 12").
- **Historical Context Statement:** Conclude or introduce the citation with a brief statement explaining _why_ this source and this moment are historically significant (e.g., "This match unified the WWF and WCW Championships, effectively ending the Monday Night Wars storyline").

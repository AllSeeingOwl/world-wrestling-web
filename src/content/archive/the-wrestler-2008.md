_The Wrestler_ is a film directed by Darren Aronofsky and written by Robert Siegel. It premiered at the 65th Venice Film Festival in September 2008 and won the festival's top prize, the Golden Lion, the following day. Mickey Rourke stars as Randy "The Ram" Robinson, a wrestler who headlined arenas in the 1980s and now works small independent shows, alongside Marisa Tomei and Evan Rachel Wood.

## The Homage

The film is set inside the real world of independent wrestling. It shows the backstage planning of matches, the friendships between opponents, the self-inflicted cuts and painkillers, and the autograph signings where former stars meet their remaining fans. Randy's story, a fading performer who cannot give up the ring despite a heart condition, draws on the experiences of many real wrestlers of his generation.

It meets the charter's criteria for Homage & Influence through both its narrative and its close attention to wrestling's visual and working culture.

## Why It Matters

_The Wrestler_ took the subject of professional wrestling to film festivals and awards ceremonies. Both Rourke and Tomei received Academy Award nominations. It remains one of the most prominent serious dramas about wrestling, and its picture of the physical toll of the business echoed the concerns raised by documentaries such as _Beyond the Mat_, also in this registry.

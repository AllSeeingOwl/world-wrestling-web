On 4 January 1999, _WCW Monday Nitro_ aired live from the Georgia Dome in Atlanta. The main event was scheduled as Kevin Nash, the WCW World Heavyweight Champion, against Hollywood Hogan. When the bell rang, Hogan poked Nash in the chest, Nash fell to the mat, and Hogan pinned him to win the title. The two then embraced, reuniting the nWo factions.

## The Context

By the start of 1999, WCW's long ratings lead over the WWF had ended, and _Raw_ was gaining momentum. That night, _Raw_ was airing a pre-taped episode in which Mick Foley, as Mankind, would win the WWF Championship from The Rock for the first time.

## The Failures

Two decisions combined that night:

- **Giving away the rival show.** WCW knew the result of the taped _Raw_ and chose to reveal it on air. Commentator Tony Schiavone told viewers that Foley would win the title, adding that it would "put butts in seats". Many viewers switched channels to watch Foley's win, with estimates of between 500,000 and 600,000. _Raw_ won the ratings that night.
- **The title change itself.** A heavily promoted championship match was turned into a staged finish that rewarded the audience's investment with nothing. It also reset the nWo storyline back to where it had been years earlier.

This is a **Contextual Failure**: both choices misjudged an audience that was already losing faith in WCW's storytelling, and both helped the competitor more than WCW itself.

## Lessons for the Craft

The night is now the standard example of two lessons: a championship is only as valuable as the matches fought over it, and a promotion that tries to undercut a rival can end up promoting it. WCW never regained the ratings lead. Foley himself later described the Fingerpoke as a "dreadful creative decision", but said giving away the end of _Raw_ was the worse mistake.

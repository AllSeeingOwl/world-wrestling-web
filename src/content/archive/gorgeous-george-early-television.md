George Wagner, wrestling as Gorgeous George, made his first television appearance on 11 November 1947. Over the following years he became one of the most recognisable figures on American television, at a time when the medium itself was new. This entry covers his body of televised work from the late 1940s into the 1950s, which the charter treats as a single work of "consistent activity".

## The Act

Gorgeous George built a character designed to be hated. He wore curled, bleached hair and elaborate robes, and entered to "Pomp and Circumstance". A valet sprayed perfume in the ring before he would wrestle, and he handed out gold-coloured hairpins to the crowd. He played the vain, cowardly villain, and audiences paid and tuned in hoping to see him beaten.

## Why It Matters

Wrestling suited early television: it was cheap to produce, easy to follow and filmed in a small space. Gorgeous George gave it a star. Contemporary accounts and later historians credit him with helping to sell television sets in the medium's first years, comparing his draw to that of comedian Milton Berle.

His influence reached well beyond wrestling. Muhammad Ali, James Brown and Bob Dylan each acknowledged him as an influence on how they presented themselves in public. Ali in particular adopted the boastful, attention-seeking persona that George had shown could fill arenas.

Within wrestling, he showed that a strong character could matter as much as athletic ability. The idea of the wrestling gimmick, a larger-than-life persona built for an audience, owes much to him. He was inducted into the WWE Hall of Fame in 2010.

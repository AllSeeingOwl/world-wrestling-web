On 17 May 1963, at Madison Square Garden in New York City, Bruno Sammartino defeated Buddy Rogers for the World Wide Wrestling Federation (WWWF) World Heavyweight Championship. The match lasted under a minute; most sources give 48 seconds.

## The Context

The WWWF had recently split from the National Wrestling Alliance, and Buddy Rogers was its first champion. In April 1963, Rogers was taken to hospital several times with chest pains. The promoters, Vincent J. McMahon and Toots Mondt, decided to move the championship to Sammartino quickly. The company's official history describes Rogers winning the title in a tournament in Rio de Janeiro, but historians regard that tournament as fictional.

Sammartino, who had emigrated from Italy as a boy and grown up in Pittsburgh, was already a strong draw with New York's large Italian-American audience.

## Why It Matters

The short match began the longest championship reign in the company's history: 2,803 days, almost eight years. Sammartino became the centre of the WWWF's business, headlining Madison Square Garden again and again and drawing crowds from immigrant communities across the north-east of the United States.

His reign set the model for the promotion's future: a single, heroic champion at the top of the card, built up over years. The WWWF later became the WWF and then WWE, and that approach can be traced through the long reigns of later champions. Sammartino was inducted into the WWE Hall of Fame in 2013.

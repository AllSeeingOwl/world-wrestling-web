On 6 November 1994, at the Los Angeles Memorial Sports Arena, Eddie Guerrero and Art Barr of Los Gringos Locos faced El Hijo del Santo and Octagón in a two-out-of-three-falls lucha de apuestas. Guerrero and Barr put up their hair; Santo and Octagón put up their masks. Santo and Octagón won two falls to one, and Guerrero and Barr had their heads shaved afterwards.

## The Context

The match grew out of a broken partnership. Guerrero had teamed with El Hijo del Santo, but the team split after Guerrero, influenced by Barr, turned on his former partner. Guerrero and Barr formed Los Gringos Locos, among the most hated villains in Mexican wrestling at the time.

When Worlds Collide was co-produced by AAA and World Championship Wrestling (WCW) and broadcast on pay-per-view in the United States, giving many American fans their first full look at a major lucha libre card.

## The Match

The match followed lucha libre traditions: three falls, rapid tags, and constant movement in and out of the ring. In an apuestas match the stakes are personal and permanent, because a luchador who loses their mask can never wear it again. The crowd's investment in protecting the masks of two of Mexico's most beloved técnicos gave every near-fall extra weight.

## Why It Matters

The match is a showcase for the "Universal Language" test in the registry's charter: its story of loyalty, betrayal and honour is clear without commentary in any language. It also influenced the American industry, as WCW signed several of the luchadores who appeared on the card over the following years.

It was Art Barr's last major match. He died on 23 November 1994, seventeen days later, at the age of 28. Guerrero later adopted Barr's frog splash as his own finishing move in tribute to him.

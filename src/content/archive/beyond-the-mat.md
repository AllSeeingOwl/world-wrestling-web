_Beyond the Mat_ is a feature documentary written, directed, produced and narrated by Barry W. Blaustein. Dated in most sources to 1999, it opened in American cinemas on 17 March 2000. It is the registry's first entry under the charter's Expanded Media criteria, which cover influential journalism and documentaries about the wrestling industry.

## The Film

Blaustein, a lifelong fan, followed three wrestlers at very different points in their careers:

- **Mick Foley**, at the height of his fame in the WWF as Mankind.
- **Terry Funk**, a veteran in his fifties who was weighing up retirement.
- **Jake "The Snake" Roberts**, a former star working smaller shows while struggling with addiction and with his relationships with his family.

The film's most discussed scene shows Foley's wife and young children at ringside during his "I Quit" match against The Rock at the 1999 Royal Rumble, as he takes repeated chair shots to the head. Blaustein later showed Foley the footage of his family's reactions, and Foley's response is captured in the film.

## The Dispute

The WWF initially gave Blaustein's crew backstage access, then withdrew its support. The company objected to the film's advertising, including its tagline, "The movie Vince McMahon doesn't want you to see!", and challenged its television ad campaign.

## Why It Matters

_Beyond the Mat_ reached a general cinema audience at the peak of wrestling's late-1990s popularity, and it showed viewers what the broadcasts left out: injury, ageing, addiction and the strain on wrestlers' families. It helped open the way for the many wrestling documentaries that followed, and its images of performers taking unprotected chair shots to the head now form part of the wider history of how attitudes to head injuries in wrestling have changed.

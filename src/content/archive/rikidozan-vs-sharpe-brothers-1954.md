On 19 February 1954, the Japan Pro Wrestling Alliance (JWA) opened a three-day series of shows in Tokyo, headlined by tag matches between Rikidōzan and judo champion Masahiko Kimura and the visiting Canadian brothers Ben and Mike Sharpe, who were billed as world tag team champions. The first night was broadcast by both NHK and Nippon Television (NTV), and NTV also broadcast the following nights.

## The Context

Rikidōzan, a former sumo wrestler, had learned professional wrestling in the United States and founded the JWA on his return to Japan in 1953. Television broadcasting had only just begun in Japan, and very few households owned a set. NTV placed televisions in public spaces, known as "street television", so that people could watch for free.

## Why It Matters

Thousands of people gathered around the street televisions to watch Rikidōzan, and the matches became a national event. Less than a decade after the Second World War, the sight of a Japanese wrestler standing up to larger American and Canadian opponents carried a strong emotional charge for its audience.

The broadcasts did as much for television in Japan as for wrestling: they showed how live sport could draw a mass audience. Rikidōzan became one of the most famous people in the country, and professional wrestling, or puroresu, became a fixture of Japanese television.

His legacy shaped the next half-century of Japanese wrestling. Two of his students, Giant Baba and Antonio Inoki, went on to found All Japan Pro Wrestling and New Japan Pro-Wrestling, the promotions behind several other entries in this registry.

Sources differ on some details of the 1954 shows, including the venues for the later nights, so this entry relies on the opening night.

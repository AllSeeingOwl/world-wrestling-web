# **The International Professional Wrestling Heritage Registry** 

## **(Also known as "The Crabtree Catalogue")** 

### **Official Charter & Guidelines** 

### **Mission Statement** 

In recognition of professional wrestling as a unique global art form, a medium for athletic storytelling, and a significant cultural force, the International Professional Wrestling Heritage Registry (IPWHR) is established. The mission of the IPWHR is to identify, categorize, and preserve for future generations a definitive collection of wrestling's most significant moments—both triumphant and tragic. This registry will serve not only as a "hall of fame" for singular achievements but also as a vital educational archive, providing context for the industry's evolution and crucial lessons from its past. It is a living history, intended to foster a deeper appreciation of the craft and advocate for a safer, more responsible future. 

### **Governing Principles** 

- **Impartiality:** The Registry will operate independently of any single promotion, era, or style. Significance is the sole metric for consideration. 

- **Global Scope:** Wrestling is a worldwide phenomenon. The Registry will actively seek and preserve significant moments from promotions across the globe. 

- **Historical Integrity:** The Registry is committed to presenting an honest and complete history of the industry, acknowledging its greatest artistic achievements alongside its most profound ethical failures. 

- **Educational Purpose:** The primary function of this archive is to provide context and insight for fans, performers, and historians, ensuring the lessons of the past inform the future. 

### **Section I: The Registry of Excellence (The "Good Apples")** 

This is the celebratory branch of the IPWHR, dedicated to preserving moments of outstanding artistic, historical, and cultural achievement. Induction into this registry signifies a moment's enduring importance to the fabric of professional wrestling. 

##### **Criteria for Induction:** 

An entry must be deemed significant in at least one of the following three categories: 

1. **Cultural Significance:** Moments that transcended the wrestling world and entered the broader public consciousness. They reflect, or in some cases influenced, the values, trends, and conversations of their time. 

2. **Historical Significance:** Moments that fundamentally altered the professional wrestling industry. This includes pivotal business decisions, character debuts that launched new eras, influential events, or moments that marked a definitive "passing of the torch." 

3. **Aesthetic Significance:** Moments recognized for their exceptional artistic and athletic merit. This includes matches, promos, or angles celebrated for their brilliant in-ring storytelling, innovative technique, unique character work, or masterful execution of the craft. 

### **Section II: The Registry of Infamy** 

This is the educational and cautionary branch of the IPWHR. Its purpose is not to mock or ridicule, but to preserve moments that offer invaluable lessons in what to avoid. It is divided into two distinct divisions based on the nature of the failure. 

#### **Division A: Failures in Craft (The "Bad Apples")** 

For moments that failed on a creative, technical, or conceptual level. These entries serve as case studies for 

performers, bookers, and producers. 

- **Conceptual Failure:** The idea was fundamentally flawed, bizarre, or nonsensical from its inception. 

- **Technical Failure:** The execution of a move, promo, or segment catastrophically collapsed due to error, accident, or poor planning. 

- **Contextual Failure:** The moment completely misjudged the audience, the cultural climate, or the narrative context, resulting in a tone-deaf or counterproductive outcome. 

- **The "So Bad It's Good" Exception:** Moments that failed so spectacularly in their intended purpose that they looped back around to become iconic and beloved pieces of wrestling lore. 

#### **Division B: The Cautionary Archive (The "Rotten Apples" / "Red Line" Registry)** 

This is the most solemn and important division of the IPWHR. Its purpose is to document profound ethical and moral failures, ensuring they are never forgotten, and their lessons are carried forward. Entry into this archive is reserved for moments that crossed a fundamental line of safety, decency, or human dignity. 

- **Exploitation of Real-Life Tragedy:** Instances where real-world suffering, injury, or death was leveraged for a storyline in a tasteless and exploitative manner. 

- **Breaches of Performer Safety & Trust:** Moments where the duty of care between performers was maliciously broken, or where a promotion's negligence led to legitimate, severe physical or psychological harm that went far beyond the understood risks of the craft. 

- **Systemic Failures & Negligence:** Broader events or patterns that exposed deep-seated, dangerous problems within the industry's culture, structure, or wellness practices, serving as a warning against institutional negligence. 

### **Section III: The Registry of Homage & Influence** 

This special registry documents the significant and demonstrable influence of professional wrestling on other forms of popular culture. Its purpose is to archive the many ways wrestling's unique aesthetic, characters, and narrative structures have "seeped into" and shaped video games, animation, film, and other media. 

##### **Criteria for Induction:** 

An entry must be a work from outside the wrestling industry that contains a clear, significant, and demonstrable element directly influenced by or paying homage to professional wrestling. This includes: 

- **Character Homages:** Characters in video games, animation, or film whose design, name, or move-set is a direct and recognizable homage to a specific wrestler or wrestling archetype (e.g., King from _Tekken_ as an homage to Tiger Mask). 

- **Narrative Structures:** Storylines or entire series built around classic wrestling tropes, such as tournament arcs, dramatic rivalries, stable warfare, or the "heel turn." 

- **Aesthetic Influence:** The adoption of wrestling's visual language, including dramatic posing, choreographed fight scenes that utilize wrestling moves, and over-the-top character introductions. 

- **Direct Parody or Satire:** Episodes or segments of a larger work that are explicitly about, or directly parody, the world of professional wrestling. 

### **Section IV: Nomination & Selection Process** 

#### **Eligibility Criteria for Nominated Works:** 

1. **Age:** A nominated work must be a **minimum of five (5) years old** from the date of its original broadcast or consistent activity. This allows time for its legacy and significance to be properly understood. There is no maximum age limit. 

2. **Verifiable Record:** The work must have been televised, live-streamed, or otherwise distributed on a professional and publicly accessible platform, ensuring a verifiable public record exists for preservation. 

3. **Universal Eligibility:** Works from any professional wrestling promotion, active or defunct, from anywhere in the world, are eligible for nomination for the Registries of Excellence and Infamy. Works for the Registry of Homage & Influence may come from any medium. 

4. **The "Universal Language" Test:** For non-English language _matches_ , strong consideration will be given to their ability to convey a compelling story and emotional drama without the need for translated commentary. This prioritizes the universal art of physical storytelling. (Promos and angles will be judged 

on their documented historical/cultural impact within their native context). 

5. **Significance Over Acclaim:** A work does not need to be critically acclaimed (e.g., a "five-star match") to be eligible. Its significance is the only prerequisite. 

6. **Composite Work Nominations:** A series of interconnected works (e.g., a match trilogy, a tournament, a complete feud) may be nominated as a single entry if their collective significance is greater than the sum of their individual parts. The nomination must clearly define the scope of the composite work. 

7. **Expanded Media Nominations:** The definition of a "work" extends beyond in-ring or televised segments. It includes other significant media from or about the wrestling industry that have had a demonstrable cultural or historical impact. This includes, but is not limited to: 

   - Content created by wrestling personalities (e.g., YouTube series, Twitch channels, podcasts). 

   - Influential works of wrestling journalism and media (e.g., interview series, documentaries, investigative reporting) created by media personalities. 

#### **The Nomination & Selection Cycle** 

The selection process is divided to reflect the distinct nature of the registries. 

##### **Process for The Registries of Excellence, Infamy (Division A), and Homage & Influence:** 

1. **Public Nomination Period:** An annual nomination period will be opened to the public. Any individual may nominate up to **fifty (50)** distinct works for consideration for these three registries. 

2. **Submission Requirements:** Each nomination must include the name of the work, the creator/promotion, the approximate date, and a brief (1-2 sentence) justification of its significance. For composite works, the scope must be clearly defined. 

3. **Board Review:** A diverse and rotating board of respected wrestling historians, veteran journalists, retired performers, and other industry experts (The Preservation Board) will review all public nominations and add their own expert selections for consideration. 

4. **Selection:** Following deliberation, the Board will make the final selections for that year's class. 5. **Annual Induction:** Each year, **up to 25 works will be inducted into the Registry of Excellence** , **up to 10 works will be inducted into Failures in Craft** , and **up to 5 works will be inducted into the Registry of Homage & Influence** . 

6. **Citation for Individual Merit:** When inducting a composite work, the Preservation Board may issue a special "Citation for Individual Merit" to a specific component (e.g., a single match, promo, or segment) within that work. This citation recognizes that the component possesses exceptional significance worthy of individual distinction. 

##### **Process for The Cautionary Archive (Division B):** 

1. **Private Deliberation:** Selections for this archive are **not open to public nomination** due to the sensitive and often traumatic nature of the events. 

2. **Board Selection:** Inductees are determined solely by an annual, private deliberation of The Preservation Board. The Board's primary objective is educational and preventative. 

3. **Annual Induction:** The number of annual inductees into this archive is not fixed and is determined by the Board's solemn deliberations. 

**Final Announcement:** The complete annual list of inductees across all registries will be announced on the first Monday of the calendar year.

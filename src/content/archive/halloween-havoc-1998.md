On 25 October 1998, World Championship Wrestling's (WCW) Halloween Havoc pay-per-view ran well past its scheduled three hours. Many cable systems ended their feeds at the scheduled time, so a large share of paying viewers lost the broadcast during the main event, in which Goldberg defended the WCW World Heavyweight Championship against Diamond Dallas Page.

## The Failure

Pay-per-view events were sold in fixed time slots, and providers did not always extend them. WCW's card ran long, and when the slot ended, many viewers' screens went dark partway through the most important match of the night. Goldberg won the match, but many of the people who had paid to see it did not.

WCW then broadcast the match free on _Monday Nitro_ the next night.

This is a **Technical Failure**: the planning of the broadcast collapsed. The show was not built to fit the time that had been sold, so the main event did not reach its audience.

## Lessons for the Craft

The night shows why running order and timing are part of the craft of a wrestling show, not just a production detail. It also shows the cost of fixing one mistake with another: giving the main event away for free on television told viewers they could skip future pay-per-views and wait for _Nitro_.

The same event's undercard included a widely criticised rematch between Hulk Hogan and The Warrior, and the night is often cited among WCW's errors in the late stage of the Monday Night Wars, alongside the Fingerpoke of Doom, also in this registry.
